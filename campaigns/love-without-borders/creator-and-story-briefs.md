## ONE SONG, DIFFERENT EXPRESSIONS
Three briefs for an opt-in conversation. No deliverable, payment, deadline or rights transfer is being agreed in these briefs. Invite an existing portfolio example and interest first, rather than unpaid speculative work. The creator retains ownership of their original contribution. Publication and music permissions require separate, specific agreement.

### Brief A: The letter in one line
For lettering artists, illustrators and poets.
Creative question: What matters more than an apology?
Starting point: An existing piece that explores accountability, or an original line the creator already wants to share. No copying song lyrics or attributing invented words to Sean.
Potential treatment, subject to agreement: reveal a handwritten line, credit its author, then provide a link to One More Chance and Sean's official channel. A silent visual with a direct listening link avoids assuming permission to synchronize the recording.
Suggested final screen: “One More Chance | Sean Austin. Discover the music on Sean Austin Reggae, YouTube.”
Website credit: https://www.seanaustinmusic.com/

### Brief B: A promise in movement
For dancers and movement storytellers.
Creative question: What does choosing someone look like without words?
Starting point: A short conversation about an existing portfolio performance, not a request to record new choreography for free.
Potential treatment, subject to agreement: a clearly credited movement interpretation paired with an approved music excerpt only after the necessary permissions are confirmed. A linked discussion of the official video is an alternative without an audio upload.
Visual tone: intentional, expressive and intimate, not a staged fan reaction or claimed spontaneous endorsement.
Listening reference: Fi Yu Forever, https://youtu.be/bXUj-hX-YWc

### Brief C: One promise, one place
For wedding storytellers, couples' writers and photographers.
Creative question: What is one promise that matters after the celebration?
Starting point: A voluntarily shared sentence and an existing image only where the couple and photographer have approved the specific use. Participation is not contingent on marital status, buying a service or following a channel.
Potential treatment: the couple's approved sentence, an optional location at country level, full agreed credits, and a link or embed of Fi Yu Forever. Do not use a real client's wedding as stock promotion.

## EDITORIAL INTERVIEW PACKAGE
Working headline: The apology and the promise: Sean Austin on love across borders.
Introduction: Jamaican reggae artist Sean Austin, based in Houston, brings accountability and commitment into the same conversation through One More Chance and Fi Yu Forever. This interview invites a discussion of the stories behind the songs and the meaning listeners bring to them.

Questions for an actual interview, not fabricated answers:
1. What was the first emotional idea behind One More Chance?
2. How do you distinguish asking for forgiveness from taking responsibility?
3. What promise sits at the heart of Fi Yu Forever for you?
4. How has living in Houston shaped the way you carry Jamaican identity into your music?
5. What do you hope someone who does not speak Jamaican patois still feels when listening?
6. What does collaboration with a producer bring to how a story becomes a record?
7. What would you like a couple to hear in Fi Yu Forever beyond the first dance?
8. What would you tell someone discovering your catalogue for the first time?
