# THE APOLOGY & THE PROMISE
## Four complete host-led radio segments

These scripts can be read by the station's own presenter. They do not require Sean to record a new intro, host a programme or appear live. The station chooses whether to use them and supplies its normal licensed broadcasting arrangements. Do not imply that the written kit grants blanket music rights. Run as separate segments or choose one. Do not pressure listeners to reconcile, reveal identifying details or make public allegations.

## Segment 1: What changes after an apology?
Track: One More Chance, Sean Austin. Producer: XO.

OPEN:
“An apology can start a conversation, but it cannot finish the work for us. Tonight's question is simple: what would you need to see change before giving someone another chance? Sean Austin takes that question into reggae with One More Chance.”

TRANSITION:
“Think about a principle rather than a person's name. Patience? Honesty? Keeping a small promise? Share a short answer through our usual listener channel if you would like to join the conversation.”

PLAY: The station-selected approved recording of One More Chance. Retrieve the correct broadcast master before scheduling; do not rip third-party audio.

AFTER THE SONG:
“That was Sean Austin, One More Chance. Another chance is a choice, not something anyone is owed. What does rebuilding trust look like to you?”

CLOSE:
“Discover more from Sean Austin on YouTube: Sean Austin Reggae. His official website is seanaustinmusic.com.”

## Segment 3: A dedication across distance
Track: Fi Yu Forever, Sean Austin. Producer: Troyton Music.

OPEN:
“Some of the people closest to us are listening from far away. This next song is for the promises that survive a time-zone difference. Wherever you are listening, here is Sean Austin with Fi Yu Forever.”

OPTIONAL LISTENER INVITATION:
“Send a first name or alias and a short dedication through our usual channel. A country is optional. Keep addresses, phone numbers and private family details out of the message.”

PLAY: Fi Yu Forever.

AFTER THE SONG:
“Distance changes the routine, but it does not have to define the meaning of a promise. Thank you to everyone who chose to share a dedication.”

EDITOR NOTE: Read the final thank-you only if real dedications were received. Otherwise say, “What small promise keeps you connected across distance?” Never invent a dedication or participant location.

CLOSE:
“For more music across the miles, find Sean Austin Reggae on YouTube and visit seanaustinmusic.com.”
